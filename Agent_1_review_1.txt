## (3) Mathematical rigor and depth

### Strengths

* The manuscript attempts to define objects (discursive networks) and provides closed-form stationary distributions for simple Markov models.
* The “invalidation floor” lemma is a reasonable use of KL/log-sum style inequalities (conceptually fine).

### Major rigor problems (must fix for publication)

**A. Incorrect measure-theory statement and duplicated definition (lines 340–342).**
You state `P_\theta \ll Q` and then claim “P_\theta(x) > 0 whenever Q(x) > 0,” which is the *opposite direction*. Finite `KL(Q||P_\theta)` actually requires `Q \ll P_\theta`, i.e., `P_\theta(x)>0` wherever `Q(x)>0`. This is not a nit; it undermines the logic of the “finite loss ⇒ floor” argument if written incorrectly.

**B. Notation collisions that undermine clarity**

* `q` is used both as a corpus invalidation mass (Section 3.1) and later as a self-repair probability in Markov models.
* `\lambda` is a fabrication parameter and also used as an eigenvalue variable in the characteristic polynomial (lines 773–775).

These collisions are significant for mathematical readability and should be eliminated.

**C. Conservation/normalization failures in “emergent invalidation” and cross-network models (lines 688–699; 842–848).**
Your state updates for `(T,F)` add a Poisson “fabrication” term `X` to `F` but do not subtract it from `T`, causing `T+F` to grow by `X` (unless you reinterpret T/F as “counts of statements” rather than actors, in which case your normalization `π_T=T/n` is wrong). This is the central mathematical flaw.

**D. Cross-network proof contains a plain expectation error (line 910).**
You set `E[X_k]=λ_k` despite defining `X_k ~ Poisson(λ_k T_k(t))`, whose expectation is `λ_k T_k(t)`.

**E. Ad-hoc constraint introduced to force normalization (eq. 884).**
Introducing `λ_k = d_{jk} p_k/(p_k+q_k)` solely to make proportions sum to one is a red flag: it indicates the model is not correctly specified.

### Depth comments

Even after fixing the above, the models are extremely mean-field (no topology, no correlation, no adversarial agents, no dependence on content). That’s acceptable if clearly framed as *toy models*, but you should avoid “phase transition” language unless you truly derive a bifurcation/critical phenomenon in a principled way.
